# Short 32-versus-128 simulation diagnostic — 2026-11-05

**Result: more search helps some eye-filling cases, but is not a complete fix.**
This was a fixed-checkpoint diagnostic, not training or a strength match. No
filter, feature, temperature, model, training budget or campaign was changed.

## Setup

- Latest leaky-value checkpoint: `79f776a011e48792c45e486a253d3cfda3458afab2fac5565a2f131209411fd2`.
- Eight size-9 positions: six recorded noncapturing small-eye fills from six
  distinct games, plus one recorded-pass and one other-placement control.
- Source games: the first six sorted round-10 self-play SGFs with at least 60
  moves. These games were generated by the **round-9 actor**; both budgets here
  use the final **round-10 actor**. This is not a replay of the original RNG state.
- Selection inspects the second half of each game and takes a late eye fill per
  game, excluding positions immediately after an opponent pass. The two controls
  come from the last source game. Exact source hashes and plies are retained.
- Three seeds per position, paired between 32 and 128: **48 searches**. Six
  diagnostic positions, not eighteen independent positions, underlie the main
  comparison. This is a targeted failure-conditioned sample, not a random test set.
- Unchanged defaults: cpuct 1.5, widening 2.0, exponent 0.5, proposal attempts 128,
  independent exploration 0.25; no eye guidance or rollouts.
- GTX 1080, release/mimalloc, CUDA ONNX Runtime 1.20.1, one inference thread,
  six feature threads, 64-context cache. A fresh cache per search; GPU/root warmup
  outside the timer. Alternated budget order. RNG reset per position/seed.
- The budget also controls the proposal-pool size through progressive widening,
  so these are actual 32/128 configurations, **not identical trees extended by
  exactly 96 simulations**.

Small-eye labels are applied **after search**, with capture exemption, and never
influence its actions. A second label checks whether the group containing the
placed stone is capturable by one legal opposing placement, using shared rule
analysis and a verified capture witness. That label does not prove the move is
strategically losing: the group could already be doomed or a sacrifice useful.
Benson is used only to describe root positions, never as neural input or guidance.

## Main results

Mean visit fractions over the six eye-fill positions, three seeds each:

| Metric | 32 simulations | 128 simulations |
|---|---:|---:|
| Visits to noncapturing own small-eye fills | 60.8% | 51.1% |
| Visits to eye fills leaving the placed group capturable in one | 15.8% | 9.6% |
| Visits to pass | 13.4% | 15.5% |
| Maximum-visit choice is an eye fill | 2/18 | 0/18 |
| Maximum-visit choice is pass | 9/18 | 8/18 |

The pass-choice count decreases because one seed switches from pass to another
non-eye placement; it is not by itself an accuracy measure. There is no optimal-
action oracle here. Not every geometric eye fill is necessarily a bad move.

Total timed search work across all eight positions: **6.36 s versus 23.08 s**,
about **3.63×**. Median individual search latency: 0.139 s versus 0.522 s. These
are serial, selected-position latencies, not concurrent self-play throughput.
The complete final diagnostic, including setup and post-search labels, took
**30.54 s**. A repeated run produced identical root visit policies and best moves.

Averages across the three seeds. IDs match `results-final.json`.

| ID | Game / ply | 32 | 128 | Observation |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 1 / 65 | 41.7% | 19.5% | clear reduction |
| 1 | 2 / 61 | 26.0% | 12.5% | all flagged eye fills leave the played group capturable |
| 2 | 3 / 48 | 68.8% | 45.1% | improvement, but substantial immediate-capture exposure remains |
| 3 | 4 / 50 | 79.2% | 81.5% | no improvement; root value already approximately -1 |
| 4 | 5 / 63 | 72.9% | 72.4% | pass is the largest individual action at both budgets |
| 5 | 6 / 70 | 76.0% | 75.5% | same pattern: pass wins individually, eye fills win collectively |

For the two controls, eye-fill mass is zero at both budgets. The pass control has
pass probability/visit mass one under this inference selection; the other
control chooses a non-eye placement. These are sanity checks, not strength tests.

## Important interpretation: sampling versus best move

In positions 4 and 5, every seed at both budgets picks **pass** under maximum-visit
selection. Yet approximately **three quarters of the total visits go to eye
fills**, spread over multiple individual coordinates.

Self-play currently uses `SearchResult::sample` in proportion to visits at every
turn; evaluation uses `SearchResult::best`. Therefore these positions can generate
frequent self-play eye fills even when pass is already the most-visited action.
The training target is also the full root visit distribution. More simulations
barely change that distribution in these two cases.

This does not establish that sampling explains every failure. The two cases with
one-move capture exposure still have considerable unsafe visit mass at 128, and
midgame evaluation/exploration can remain inadequate. Nor does being currently
uncapturable prove that filling an eye is harmless in the long term.

**Recommendation:** do not increase the entire campaign's search cost fourfold
on this evidence alone. A separate, cheap experiment with a generic late-game
sampling-temperature schedule is worth considering before adding a permanent
life feature or eye ban. No temperature change was made here.
